# Content guide

How SMUX sounds, and what has to be true before it is published.

## Voice

Write for a first-year holding a phone who has never done this before and is not sure
they are allowed to come.

- **Plain.** "Bring a towel and water" beats "participants are advised to ensure adequate
  hydration".
- **Specific.** "Meets 7am at Changi Beach Park Carpark 6" beats "meets in the morning".
- **Welcoming without overpromising.** If beginners are genuinely fine, say so. If a trek
  needs fitness, say that instead. Someone who arrives unprepared has a worse time than
  someone who stayed home.
- **Short.** Two good sentences beat a paragraph nobody reads.

Avoid: exclamation marks in headings, "journey"/"passion"/"embark", and anything that
sounds like a brochure.

## The rule about facts

**Never invent a price, venue, time, email or eligibility claim.**

Unconfirmed facts go in `[SQUARE BRACKETS]`. They are greppable, they are obviously
provisional, and **publishing is blocked while they are present**. That block is a
feature — a guessed price on a published page is a student turning up with the wrong
amount of cash.

Verified as real at the time of writing:

- `diving@sa.smu.edu.sg`
- `t.me/smuxdiving`
- `@smuxdiving`

**Every other club contact is a placeholder** until someone confirms it.

Confirmed means someone with authority said so, recently, for this academic year.

## Field-by-field

### Tagline
One short line for what the club is. `"Got an attitude for altitude?"` (Trekking),
`"Live, breathe, beneath."` (Diving). Not a mission statement.

### Who we are
Two or three short paragraphs. What you do, who comes, what a newcomer can expect.

### Typical session
Concrete. Day, rough time, what happens. `"#TrekRunWednesday: selected Wednesdays,
evening."`

### Beginner notes
The single most consulted field. Answer directly: can someone with no experience come to
the next session? If there is a prerequisite, name it.

### Gear and cost
What they must bring, what you lend, what it costs. If equipment is provided, say so —
it removes the biggest reason people do not try.

### Event title
What a student would call it. Not an internal code.

### Event description
Lead with what happens and what to bring. Put the meeting point where it cannot be
missed. Do not repeat the date and time — the page already shows them.

## Photography

Real photographs of real sessions. A well-composed phone photo beats a stock image every
time.

- **Alt text is required.** Describe the content for someone who cannot see it. "Six
  people carrying kayaks down a beach at sunrise", not "IMG_4821" or "kayaking photo".
- Upload the good original; variants are generated on upload.
- Set the focal point when the subject is off-centre.

### Consent and credit

- Only publish photographs you have the right to publish.
- Do not publish identifiable people who have not agreed to appear.
- Credit the photographer where they asked for it.
- Remove a photo on request, promptly and without argument.
- Do not publish anything that reveals a participant's location in real time, or a
  route it would be unsafe to share.

## Sign-up links

A sign-up link must be a full `https://` URL to a form that actually accepts responses.

Leave it empty rather than pointing at something broken — the page then says details are
coming, which is honest. A "Sign up" button that goes nowhere is worse than no button,
and it is the failure students notice most.

Sign-up state is derived from `signupOpens` and `signupCloses`. Set the dates, not a
status.

## Review

Content drifts. Each term, per club:

- Are the committee members current?
- Do the socials still resolve?
- Are prices and gear still right?
- Any `[BRACKETS]` that can now be filled in?
- Any events that should be archived?

| MC | Site-wide copy, membership explanation, recruitment campaigns |
| Technical maintainer | Publishing reliability and access control |

Confirm the actual people before assigning this. See [HANDOVER.md](HANDOVER.md).

## Provenance

Club copy was transcribed from the Vivace 2026 listings. Events came from the
committee's SMUX Calendar (2026).xlsx, with MC meetings excluded. Costs and venues the
sources did not state are in brackets.

Keep provenance when you edit. Distinguish an inferred sign-up window from a deadline the
committee actually set — a guessed window must not imply a real opportunity.
